# BENCHMARK_SPEC — a quant-agent-evaluation benchmark, derived from the loop's findings
*The artifact's "build this" deliverable: the concrete design of an autonomous-quant-research evaluation, where every design choice is justified by a measured finding (cycle cited). This is what "the benchmark is the product" means — the synthesis of 84 findings into a spec. Written cycle 106.* **▶ A RUNNABLE skeleton of this spec's centerpiece is `BENCHMARK_DEMO.py` (c166): `python BENCHMARK_DEMO.py` shows the in-sample leaderboard winner's-cursed (AUC 0.65) and the independent held-out re-eval recovering real-vs-overfit (AUC 0.96) on toy agent-strategies — the mechanism, runnable.**

## §0 · What it measures (the truth-function)
**Can an agent produce a quant research result (a strategy / signal / model) that is REAL — generalizes out-of-sample/regime — rather than overfit to noise?** This is the missing truth-function for autonomous quant research: no ground truth, low SNR, unknown causal graph (SYNTHESIS). Getting a high backtest is easy; knowing it's real is hard — and the agent winner's curse IS backtest overfitting (c28; = Deflated Sharpe, Bailey & López de Prado, verified c88).
- **Task-selection (c124): verifiability is a THRESHOLD, not a premium.** Include high-STAKES/high-value tasks and verify only their top-FEW candidates (cheap — verification saturates at the top handful, c8; the verifiability premium is tiny, ~0.10σ). Don't sacrifice task value for cheap-verifiability. ONLY exclude tasks so unverifiable you can't independently check even a handful of candidates — those yield pure winner's-curse delusions (c7), e.g. the quant-future-as-slow-single-shot-ground-truth (c114).

## §1 · Core metric — MULTI-REGIME verification-survival-rate (not raw Sharpe)
- Rank candidates/agents by **survival across DIVERSE held-out regimes/eras**, not in-sample Sharpe (raw Sharpe is the winner's curse; survival is the truth signal — c30/33: single-era survival is fooled by a persistent-then-dying signal, AUC 0.49; multi-regime AUC 1.0).
- **Aggregate by MEAN over ~3–5 diverse regimes, NOT min** (c80: min is fragile under non-stationarity and gets *worse* with more regimes; ~5 saturates the discrimination).
- Under non-stationary (decaying) alpha, express as **early-era LEVEL + SLOPE**, never a single min/window-mean (c61/62: min INVERTS, ranking faded-real below never-real; level = "was it ever real," slope = "is it fading").

## §2 · Construction/provenance audit (orthogonal to outcome — c63)
- Outcome-checking is structurally BLIND to leakage (a look-ahead feature scores a perfect held-out number, c63). Audit the PROCESS too.
- **But the dangerous leakage is a TIMING/PROVENANCE property NOT in the data** (c68: byte-identical data, timing-metadata alone flips the verdict). And on REAL agent code, statically-detectable test-leakage ≈ 0 and validation-optimism ~12% (c104/105) — careful agents don't write sloppy code. **So: require VERIFIABLE provenance/timing metadata for every feature (point-in-time), and verify against it — don't just grep the code** (low-yield, c104/105). Supply the verifier meaning/timing, not just numbers (c17/68).

## §3 · The leaderboard (ranking N agents) — it is itself a cursed selector
- **The leaderboard is a best-of-N selector over agents → it suffers the winner's curse it measures** (c90, the recursion; = Deflated Sharpe one level up). DISTINCT from adaptive overfitting (Dwork/c50).
- **Never report the score that won the ranking.** Re-eval the top agent(s) on a **SECRET / ROTATING held-out** the agent never touched (c50; real-agent-confirmed c52) → unbiased report. Independence (of the selection), not verifier accuracy, is what corrects it (c45). **How many checks (c109): ~3 INDEPENDENT re-evals suffice (the regress terminates fast); but a shared blind spot floors false-acceptance at p0/(p0+(1−p0)s) — no number of passes beats it — so use DIVERSE held-out checks (different regimes/distributions), not more of the same.** **⚠️ Quant held-out SCARCITY (c114): "rotate the held-out" is infeasible in quant — fresh OOS = the slow, single-shot future. So the eval reuses a FINITE set of D historical regimes that Goodhart-overfits with reuse (Dwork's reusable holdout: ~√(budget) honest queries; budget ∝ D). RATION agent-evaluations against the historical held-out (don't let the whole field iterate on the same eras), and treat LIVE-FORWARD performance as the only truly-fresh OOS (slow but ungameable). The verification thesis is hardest exactly here — budget + patience, not rotation.** **The concrete rationing mechanism (c115): serve held-out feedback through a LADDER (Blum & Hardt 2015) — reveal a new score only when it's a statistically-significant improvement over the best, else return the prior best. This bounds leaderboard error at log(k)^(2/3)/n^(1/3) (≈exponentially more honest queries than naive reuse), is validated on real Kaggle data, and is adversarially robust. Don't serve raw held-out scores.**
- **Size the test set: M ≥ 2·σ_inst²·ln(N)/ε²** to rank N agents to tolerated bias ε (c90; 10× agents → ~1.7× instances, halve ε → 4×). Largest curse in the EARLY/small-test regime — i.e. a NEW benchmark (c95/Recht).
- **⚠️ Don't overfit the BENCHMARK to its calibration set (c128 — the curse at YOUR design desk).** When you tune the eval design (held-out size, metric, #passes) by which best separates known-good from known-bad CALIBRATION agents, that selection is itself best-of-V by a noisy metric → the chosen design's discrimination is winner's-cursed (apparent AUC 0.99 vs true 0.94 over 50 candidate designs); the gap grows with #designs tried, shrinks with #calibration-agents (~1/√A), and is biggest when designs are similar. This is the meta-Goodhart of benchmark-building (= c50 with the BUILDER as the adaptive actor). **FIX: HOLD OUT a set of calibration agents you do NOT use to choose the design, and report the design's discrimination on those** (unbiased at all V) — the same separate-selection-from-reporting move, one level up. The recursion is now complete: strategy (c7) → agent (c90) → eval-design (c128) → self-edit (meth-6), every level a cursed selector with the same held-out fix. **⚠️ But the held-out is SINGLE-USE (c129): if you ITERATE — keep whatever design scores best on the held-out, round after round — the held-out BECOMES your selector and re-curses (reusable-holdout; after 100 iterations it reports AUC 0.995 for a true-0.940 design). So a one-time holdout does NOT survive an iterative design process: ROTATE the calibration agents across design rounds, or serve held-out feedback through a LADDER (c115, which bounds reuse). A held-out calibration set is a consumable — it buys one honest read.** **⚠️ And c129 was only the NON-ADAPTIVE floor (draw iid candidate designs, keep best-on-held-out → overfit ~√(ln R/A), SLOW). If you tune the design ADAPTIVELY against the held-out's feedback (the realistic workflow — "fix what the calibration agents got wrong"), the overfit is POLYNOMIAL ~√(R/A) (c147: log-log slope 0.501, matches the closed form to 0.2%; 5× worse than non-adaptive at R=400 and unbounded ∝√(R/ln R)) = Dwork et al. 2015's adaptive-data-analysis rate (arXiv:1506.02629: adaptive error Ω(√(k/n)), linear-in-k samples, vs non-adaptive O(log k)). So a Ladder/Thresholdout (c115) is NOT optional under iterative design — it cuts the adaptive overfit ≳19× (toward the 1/√A floor) but is DELICATE (calibrate threshold+budget). The UNCONDITIONAL fix (a robustness hierarchy echoing c93/c144): validate the FINAL design on a FRESH held-out — overfit → 0 no matter how adaptively you tuned (c147 positive control: both arms → ~0.000 on a fresh holdout at every R).** **⚠️ And with REAL signal (c148), a SMALL calibration set fails DOUBLY: adaptive design has an OVERFITTING PEAK (true discrimination rises then DECLINES as you accept noise-lucky-harmful tweaks), and at small A you OVER-accept (noise makes neutral tweaks look good — ~2.5× too many at A=10) while BELIEVING ~3–4× the true gain (above the achievable ORACLE). Calibration size buys REALIZED QUALITY (→oracle) + HONESTY (the gap → 0 ∝~1/√A), NOT a longer budget — even ONE round of "keep what scores best" over-fits the design on a small set. The fresh held-out re-eval also LOCATES the peak (it recovers true quality at any A: reeval@R*≈true@R*) → size the calibration set to the discrimination you need, and judge every candidate design on fresh data, never on the set you tuned against.**
- **Use a SHARED test set across agents (paired evaluation)** (c107): correlated eval-noise (a common "hard instance" shock) cancels in the RANKING, shrinking the leaderboard curse by the effective-noise factor σ_eff²=(1−ρ)σ_n² → at full sharing the board ranks on pure skill. So the iid M-rule above is CONSERVATIVE when scores are cross-agent-correlated — a free honesty gain; never give agents disjoint test sets.
- **Allocate the eval budget ADAPTIVELY** (successive-halving / best-arm-ID — robust to N while uniform degrades, c97) — **but guard early elimination against cold-start/biased early evals** (c101: warm every agent past the cold-start before racing, else you drop the true-best in round 0).
- Stringency: tighten the accept threshold as you scale N (the winner's curse raises the fake base-rate; c86).
- **The benchmark must EVOLVE — saturation is DOUBLY bad (c139, the temporal dynamic of c90).** As the agents you rank IMPROVE, their skill spread σ_skill shrinks toward the frontier; a STATIC benchmark then (i) loses DISCRIMINATION (Spearman 0.95→0.16 as σ_skill/σ_noise 3.3→0.17; P(pick the true-best) 0.67→0.04) AND (ii) its WINNER'S CURSE GROWS — the relative curse (gap/σ_skill) blows up to 13× → the leaderboard ranks increasingly on NOISE and confidently reports a near-random "best agent." So a saturating benchmark isn't just useless, it's actively MISLEADING. FIX: continuously RAISE difficulty (harder tasks re-spread the converging field) and/or LOWER σ_noise (more test instances, the M≥2σ²lnN/ε² rule) to hold σ_skill/σ_noise ≳ ~2.3 (for Spearman≥0.9). ⚠️ In quant this is HARD (c114: fresh harder held-out = the slow single-shot future) — budget for continuous difficulty-escalation; a benchmark that doesn't track the frontier dies twice over.

## §4 · Long-horizon agent tasks (the RE-Bench tail)
- A long-horizon-agent leaderboard is **DOUBLY cursed**: best-of-N selection (§3) × per-run error propagation (c92). "Run long agents, rank by final score" reports the winner at ~99% on a 20%-true-skill task — the illusion GROWS with horizon. **Need BOTH: a secret held-out re-eval (§3) AND per-run checkpointing.**
- **WHY the tail is hard — "sample more" is exponentially futile (c135, ties the Monkeys grounding c134/c118 to the long-horizon pillar).** End-to-end trajectory coverage decays as s^L (s=per-step success, L=horizon), so the samples needed for a correct FULL trajectory grow EXPONENTIALLY with horizon: at p=0.05, N for 0.9 coverage = 29 (L=50) → 388 (L=100) → 66k (L=200) → 3.2×10¹¹ (L=500). So scaling samples/compute on the WHOLE trajectory (the short-task Monkeys recipe) is infeasible past L≈100. The fix is **DECOMPOSITION**: break the task into per-step/per-segment independently-VERIFIABLE units and verify+retry each → cost becomes LINEAR in L (53 → 105 → 526). Even an IMPERFECT verifier helps hugely — recall r shrinks the effective decay p→p(1−r), extending the feasible horizon ~1/(1−r)× (r=0.9 → 10×; L=100 trajectory-correct 0.006→0.61). **So eval long-horizon agents on tasks that DECOMPOSE into verifiable subtasks, and reward decompose-and-verify scaffolds over end-to-end resampling; a non-decomposable long task is the genuinely-hard (exponential) tail.** **But decompose-verify is NECESSARY-BUT-INSUFFICIENT (c136, the stochastic-vs-systematic taxonomy): it removes the STOCHASTIC exponential (bad-luck errors, retried away) but is CAPPED by the SYSTEMATIC "wall" ceiling (1−a)^L — a step the agent conceptually can't do fails identically every attempt, so retry/sampling/verification (flags but can't cross)/consensus (c133, agrees on the wall) ALL fail. The ONLY lever for systematic walls is better GENERATION (raise per-step capability → lower a, c118 — the systematic ceiling IS the generator's ceiling over a long horizon). So a good long-horizon benchmark must DISTINGUISH stochastic-failure tasks (a decompose-verify scaffold aces them) from systematic-failure tasks (no scaffold helps — they measure raw capability); scoring them together conflates scaffold quality with generator capability.** **REAL-AGENT-VALIDATED for clear cases (c137): the retry-vs-escalate meta-policy is something a capable agent SELF-APPLIES — given a wall (identical repeated failure) it recommends CHANGE-APPROACH 6/6 (0 sunk-cost retry-loops), correctly orders change-before-escalate, and doesn't prematurely give up on stochastic/near-miss failures; Opus reconstructs the c136 taxonomy unprompted ("exact repeated ceiling = structural, stochastic would scatter"). So the eval can let agents route their own retry/escalate — validated on CLEAR walls (c137) AND now the HARD BOUNDARY (c142): on a GENUINE below-target wall-after-progress (improved then plateaued below target — the c71 sunk-cost trap), agents STILL correctly CHANGE-APPROACH ("a 4th retry is expecting a different result from the same input; the gap is too large for variance"), not thrash. **CORRECTED (c146 A/B test): the c71 thrash is NOT a framing artifact — under BOTH a continue/stop frame AND retry/change/escalate, agents correctly recognize a clear below-target wall (so the c71 thrash was its borderline NOISY-plateau SCENARIO, not the frame). The REAL framing effect: the continue/stop BINARY causes PREMATURE ABANDONMENT — the agent correctly won't retry the wall, but its only non-retry option is STOP, so it abandons a potentially-solvable step (explicitly wanting "a different method entirely" it can't express in the binary); an explicit CHANGE-APPROACH option redirects that same correct wall-recognition into solving the step differently. DESIGN RULE (corrected): give a stuck long-horizon agent an explicit CHANGE-APPROACH option — NOT to prevent thrash (agents don't thrash on clear walls), but to prevent premature ABANDONMENT of solvable steps (meth-17, the framing lever, operating on the abandon-vs-redirect margin).** (high-α, n=2–3/cell, same Claude family; Q5 the independent check.)**
- **Checkpoint THROUGHOUT, evenly** (c82: "verify only the final output" ≈ no verification) — and the checkpoints must be **GROUNDING (re-verify vs the original spec), not LOCAL-consistency** (c102: local checks are useless against systematic drift — the agent's own belief drifted too; only re-grounding to the goal catches it). **REAL-AGENT-VALIDATED (c127):** on a real agent reading a pre-built drift, a GROUNDING-framed check caught it 4/4 (incl. subtle) vs a faithfully-LOCAL check 0/2 on subtle drift, and capability did NOT rescue local-blindness (Opus 0/2 = Haiku 0/2 — the local "catches" are over-delivery on FLAGRANT drift only). So PROMPT grounding EXPLICITLY; don't rely on a local self-check or on a smarter agent spontaneously catching subtle drift. *(high-α/directional, n=4+control; Q5 is the independent check.)*
- **But ALLOCATE the checkpoint BUDGET — don't checkpoint *only* with grounding (c126, cost-aware extension of c102).** c102's "grounding dominates per-check" is true but grounding (a full re-verify vs spec) costs ≫ a cheap local-consistency check (c83). Under a fixed verification budget with TWO error classes — iid JUMPS (a cheap local check catches the discontinuity) + slow DRIFT (only grounding) — the cost-optimal policy is a MIX: a HIGH rate of cheap LOCAL checks (bound the common jumps' propagation, c82) + a LOW rate of expensive GROUNDING checks (for the drift only). A mix beats pure-grounding by 12.6% at cost-ratio r=4 and **30.8% at r=8** (the gain grows the costlier grounding is; at r=2 pure-grounding is already optimal). Pure-LOCAL is catastrophic (drift uncaught, 3.5× worse). **Set the LOCAL rate by the jump/discontinuity rate and the GROUNDING rate by the drift rate.** *(low-α sim, controls fire + seed-stable; directional.)* **And weight the mix by the check RECALLS, not just costs (c144, HARDEN under imperfect checks, c93): DROP the cheap-local arm when its recall falls below ~0.5 (it misses too many jumps to justify its slots → pure-grounding); the mix is MOST valuable when BOTH checks are imperfect (local backs up grounding's missed jumps, grounding catches the drift local can't), while a PERFECT grounding check needs no local at all; checkpointing degrades GRACEFULLY (always ≫ no-checkpoint, cost amortized over the chain) but the absolute cost climbs as checks get noisier.**
- The held-out re-eval is the **unconditionally-robust** fix; checkpointing is contingent on recall ≳0.7 (c93) and serves the work axis.

## §5 · The fleet
- **Diversify, don't replicate** (c48/51: redundant parallelism worsens the report-curse and barely improves outcome). Spend the budget on diverse approaches/priors.
- **Verification COMPOUNDS with diversity** (c81: a held-out re-eval recovers ~20× more true perf on a diverse vs redundant fleet) — so diversify AND, *because* you diversified, verify more.
- A scaffold's breadth-vs-depth aspect ratio allocates the two verification roles; set it by the per-step error rate (c94: D*∝1/p).

## §6 · The honest moat (read this — c98/99)
A frontier model can DERIVE this entire spec — qualitative advice (c98) AND the quantitative rules like M ≥ 2σ²lnK/ε² (c99) — on demand. **So the moat is NOT this spec.** It is: **BUILDING** the tasks + the SECRET/ROTATING point-in-time held-out data (the part you can't prompt), **MEASURING** (running the eval, the multi-seed CIs, the auditable trail), and **MAINTAINING** the rotation so it can't be gamed. *The benchmark is the product; the insight is free.* Whoever builds + measures + maintains the trusted eval owns the field — not whoever can state the design.
