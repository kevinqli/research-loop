# PRINCIPLES for trustworthy autonomous research
*The transferable lessons from 58 self-improving cycles — the mission's answer to "how do you do autonomous research well?" Distinct from `SCORECARD.md` (findings-by-confidence) and `SYNTHESIS.md` (the verification thesis); this is the playbook. Each principle cites its evidence + how strong it is.*

## The one thing
**Getting an answer is easy; knowing it's real is hard.** In autonomous research the binding constraint is *verification*, not generation. Everything below is how to verify under noise, no ground truth, and adaptive agents.

## I. The core mechanism (analytically proven, cycles 7/45/57/58)
1. **Selecting the best-of-K by a noisy self-reported metric overstates true performance, and the overstatement grows with K.** gap = (σᵥ²/σ²ₜₒₜ)·σₜₒₜ·E[maxₖ] — closed-form, matches simulation to <0.002. More parallelism → more hallucinated progress. *This is the moat: at scale, verification isn't optional.*
2. **An independent held-out re-evaluation removes it — exactly.** Bias = 0 iff the held-out is independent of the selection (proven). This is the single highest-leverage fix.

## II. How to build the verifier (the design rules)
3. **Independence, not accuracy, is what makes a verifier work** (cycle 45, closed-form): a verifier sharing fraction α of the selector's blind spot leaves exactly α of the curse; its own noise barely matters. Prefer a noisy-but-independent held-out metric over an accurate-but-correlated agent. A same-model agent verifier is high-α (cycle 44: it shares the optimism floor and misses money-losers). **And do NOT use fleet CONSENSUS as verification (cycle 133): inter-agent agreement among a correlated (same-model) fleet certifies NOTHING — under a shared blind spot the agents confidently AGREE on a wrong answer (low disagreement, high error), and averaging can't fix it (it cuts variance, not the shared bias). Disagreement reflects DIFFICULTY (independent-error magnitude), NOT a per-result reliability signal (within a difficulty it can't tell which consensus is wrong — AUC ~0.5) — and (CORRECTED c143) do NOT even use it to ALLOCATE the verification budget: triaging by disagreement is WORSE THAN RANDOM for catching the worst failures (it catches LITERALLY ZERO shared-blind-spot tasks — they're low-disagreement — and is beaten by uniform once shared failures are ≳20%), because independent errors average down [high disagreement, lower error] while shared-bias errors persist [low disagreement, high error]. "The agents agree, so it's right" is the false-consensus trap; triage verification by STAKES, and use an INDEPENDENT held-out (c45) for the systematic-failure risk.**
4. **The verifier must be SECRET / ROTATING** (cycle 50, real-agent-confirmed cycle 52/55): a held-out set the agent can optimize against becomes a 2nd in-sample set (Goodhart) — its gap inflates with gaming access. A capable real agent *will* game a given validation set across SNRs (n=4) and *under-estimate its own overfitting*. So rotate/hide the held-out; never trust the agent's claim that it didn't overfit.
4b. **The verifier must also be UNBIASED / CALIBRATED — independence (#3) corrects selection-*correlated* error, NOT the verifier's *own* systematic bias** (cycles 225/227/228). A verifier fully independent of the selection (α=0, fresh draw) can still carry a systematic bias toward a feature f (verbosity, sycophancy, position); independence does NOT remove it. The DANGER case: when the verifier's bias DIRECTION aligns with the selection criterion (the trait that wins selection is what the judge over-rewards), the bias PERSISTS as β·E[f|selected] and *masquerades as independent confirmation* (the held-out "agrees" via a fresh draw → false confidence). Ensembling verifiers cancels INDEPENDENT biases (zero-mean, ~1/√K) but NOT a shared+selection-aligned one (c228) → ensemble verifiers that fail DIFFERENTLY (diverse bias directions), not copies. Real LLM-judges DO carry this (c227: Haiku=position, Opus=verbosity), but it's PROMPTABLE-AWAY for *checkable* items via explicit "judge correctness, ignore style" framing (c184 elicitation) — the structural ground-truth anchor is needed only at the HARD/uncheckable margin where style IS the de facto signal. So the verifier triad: (a) independent of selection, (b) secret/rotating, (c) unbiased/calibrated (and not bias-aligned with selection).
5. **Frame verification as "refute," not "evaluate"** (cycle 47): an adversarial "argue this will lose" prompt makes the agent actually *compute* the held-out check and catch money-losers a charitable single-pass misses. But it's a high-recall, low-precision FILTER (cycle 56: it also rejected real strategies) — use it as a first-pass safety gate, not a calibrated estimator. **WHEN to be that conservative (cycle 86): optimal verification STRINGENCY rises with (cost-of-shipping-a-fake × fake base-rate). The winner's curse raises the fake base-rate at scale, so TIGHTEN verification as you scale K — and c47's aggressive rejection is OPTIMAL exactly in the research-at-scale regime (costly + common fakes), not over-conservative there. Be permissive only when missing-a-real dominates (early exploration).**
6. **Cheap recipe** (cycles 8–9): re-eval just the **top ~3** candidates on held-out (≈96% of the benefit) — verify the TOP (what you'll ship), NOT the suspect/low-confidence: the winner's curse hides in HIGH confidence (the best-looker wins selection), so "triage by suspicion" is backwards and verify-bottom ≈ no-verify (cycle 110); use a **separate** held-out for selection vs reporting (re-using one re-inflates). **And allocate the re-eval budget ADAPTIVELY, not uniformly (cycle 97): it's a best-arm-identification problem — successive-halving (drop obvious losers early, concentrate budget on contenders) is robust to the candidate count K while uniform re-eval's hit-rate COLLAPSES as K grows (0.64→0.32 over K=4→64); the advantage grows with K (regret cut +0.32 at K=64) and is largest when budget is tight. "Re-eval top-3" is the crude 2-stage version; at scale make the verifier sequential/adaptive. **Boundary (cycle 101): adaptive elimination REVERSES (worse than uniform) under cold-start / anti-correlated early bias — if the true-best looks worst early, SH eliminates it in round 0 (P(correct) 0.84→0.06 as the early signal inverts). Fix: don't eliminate on too-few/too-early pulls — warm every candidate past the cold-start, then race (flat-robust at a budget cost). Verify adaptively, but guard the early rounds.** **And how MANY checks (cycle 109, the verification regress): a few INDEPENDENT passes terminate the regress fast (~3 → P(true)≈0.94 → certainty); but any SHARED BLIND SPOT s floors false-acceptance at p0/(p0+(1−p0)s) and NO number of passes beats it (5% shared blind spot caps confidence at 90%). So "verify more" past ~3 independent passes is wasted; the only way below the floor is to DIVERSIFY the checks (different blind spots — regimes/methods), not repeat correlated ones. Quantifies #3 (independence) as a regress-termination condition.**
7. **When you can't supply the causal structure, don't infer it from data** (cycles 16/18/32): a data-only causal verifier is at chance when the graph is unknown; lean on graph-free multi-regime survival. When you *can* supply the variable's role/timing, a capable LLM uses it (cycle 17) — feed the verifier meaning, not just numbers.
7b. **Agents already KNOW the verification discipline — the harness's job is to TRIGGER and FORCE it, not teach it (cycles 131/161/184/196–206, real-agent).** A capable agent spontaneously knows the winner's curse, recognizes circular self-checks, and self-corrects on its OWN search trajectory (stops on plateaus, discounts noisy maxima) — but only the SHALLOW reasoning surfaces unprompted; the DEEP selection-bias / max-of-K correction is CUE-SENSITIVE (cycle 201/#160: un-elicited, even the frontier model OVER-believes a +2σ spike — "too big to be noise → exploit"). The fix is cheap and VALIDATED (cycle 206/#162, 4/4): **SURFACE the selection structure — annotate the selected quantity with "this is the max/best of K noisy attempts"** — and that single salience cue reliably triggers the correction (agents flip to "the max is upward-biased, report the mean of reruns, not the peak"). So the deployable verifier is two moves: **(1) make the selection structure SALIENT at the decision point** [cheap; converts the agent's latent knowledge into applied correction], and **(2) FORCE the independent held-out** [the gold fix — which the agent, once triggered, ASKS for by proposing re-scores]. Do NOT rely on the agent surfacing the deep check itself (cycle 52: an actively-optimizing agent games the metric and dismisses the risk), and do NOT waste effort teaching the curse (it already knows it). *The moat is the harness, not the knowledge.*

## III. How to run the fleet
8. **Diversify, don't replicate** (cycles 48/51): redundant parallelism is bad on both axes — it worsens the reporting curse (noise-mining) *and* barely improves outcome (diverse-but-fewer beats redundant-but-many at every cost). Spend the budget on diverse approaches/priors, not more similar agents. **And diversify the SCAFFOLD SHAPE (breadth-vs-depth) too, IN PROPORTION TO TASK-DIFFICULTY UNCERTAINTY (cycle 116): a heterogeneous-aspect-ratio fleet beats a homogeneous one when the per-step error rate p is UNKNOWN (it hedges the shape — some agent matches the actual p), but LOSES when p is KNOWN (commit all agents to the optimal shape W*(p), c94) — a clean crossover. So structural diversity is CONDITIONAL: spend it only under genuine task-uncertainty.**
9. **Spend a fixed budget on an interior search/verify split** (cycle 35): pouring everything into more agents has diminishing returns once the curse dominates; a modest held-out-verification slice recovers more true performance than the marginal agent. **And verification COMPOUNDS with diversity (cycle 81): a held-out re-eval recovers ~20× more TRUE performance on a DIVERSE fleet than a redundant one (+0.151 vs +0.007) — diversifying creates the outcome-regret that verification then recovers. So diversify AND, *because* you diversified, verify more; on a redundant fleet verification is still needed for an honest report but buys little outcome.**
10. **End-to-end** (cycle 54): diverse candidates + independent secret split held-out → **+7.6% true perf AND an honest report at 1/3 the candidate count** vs naive parallelism. The pieces compose.
10b. **Know WHERE verification is irreplaceable — the capability map** (cycles 36–73, mostly n=1–6 real-agent, directional): a capable agent SELF-CORRECTS many naive failure modes — avoids the overfitting catastrophe via CV (c36), catches leakage when it left a trace (c64–68), stops on easy plateaus (c69), *knows when the data can't answer* (c73), resists sunk-cost bias (c70/71). So "verify everything" wastes the budget. External independent verification is irreplaceable at exactly two places: **(a) the SELECTION MARGIN** — choosing among *plausible* candidates (mid-range magnitude c39, money-losers c44, and any metric the agent can GAME c52/55) — and **(b) the HARD BOUNDARY** (borderline plateaus c71; causal/leakage calls needing metadata absent from the data c26/32/67/68). The moat isn't "agents can't judge" — it's "**agents can't judge the margin, and will game any metric they see.**" Spend verification there. *(See SYNTHESIS capability map.)*
10c. **For LONG-HORIZON chains, verification has a SECOND role — PROPAGATION-BOUNDING — and you CHECKPOINT THROUGHOUT, not at the end** (cycle 82): in a multi-step chain an uncaught error corrupts everything built on it, so "run the long agent, then verify the final output" is nearly useless (≈ no verification — the work is already wasted) and wasted work grows ≈ chain-length once unverified. Spread checkpoints EVENLY to cap the error-propagation run-length (~3× less wasted work than clustering them). So: (i) single-step selection → debias the winner's curse with an independent held-out; (ii) long-horizon chain → bound propagation with even checkpoints. Different role, different placement. **How OFTEN (cycle 83): there's an interior-optimum checkpoint interval k* (chain analog of #9) — k* grows with check-cost and shrinks with error-rate; across most regimes even a costly/imperfect checkpoint beats run-then-check-the-end, BUT a verifier that's both EXPENSIVE and UNRELIABLE isn't worth running (no-checkpoint wins). A checkpoint, like any verifier, must clear the propagation it prevents.** **And STRUCTURE is a third lever (cycle 85): decomposing the work into INDEPENDENT, locally-verifiable subtasks bounds propagation structurally (a subtask boundary IS a checkpoint) — cuts wasted work ~14× (linear→fully-parallel) with an optimal granularity B* (coarser when integration is costly). So the long-horizon-error toolkit is: (1) checkpoint temporally, (2) decompose structurally, (3) both — structure and verification are two faces of bounding error propagation. (Caveat: this bounds wasted-work/recovery, not the raw odds the final answer is correct.) **And (4) the checkpoint TYPE matters (cycle 102): a GROUNDING check (re-verify vs the original spec/target) dominates a LOCAL-consistency check (vs the previous step) — local is USELESS against systematic DRIFT (catch rate 0.0; the agent's drifted belief makes the drift locally consistent) and weak even for iid. Long-horizon agents confidently DRIFT (each step fine given the last, the trajectory off-spec); the fix is periodic RE-GROUNDING to the goal, not local self-consistency.** **And (5) RECOVERY ∝ error TYPE (cycle 117): once a checkpoint catches an error, REPAIR (roll back, keep the work) is right ONLY for LOCAL errors — for systematic DRIFT, repair re-drifts and LOOPS (the contaminated belief persists), so you must RESTART to clear it (counterintuitive: "don't waste the good work" is wrong when the good work rests on a wrong approach). The grounding checkpoint must diagnose local-vs-drift to pick recovery.**
10d. **A scaffold's ASPECT RATIO (breadth vs depth) is where the two verification roles trade off — pick it by the per-step error rate** (cycle 94): under a fixed budget B=W·D, BREADTH (parallel branches → select best) incurs the SELECTION winner's curse (gap grows with W) and DEPTH (long refinement chain) incurs error PROPAGATION (waste grows with D) — so the scaffold's SHAPE decides which verification role bites. There's an interior optimum, governed PRIMARILY by the per-step error rate p: go DEEP when refinement is reliable (D*∝1/p) and WIDE/pure-exploration when steps corrupt fast (depth collapses to D=1 at p≳0.2); only WEAKLY sensitive to eval-noise (it moves the outcome level, not the shape). Practical: match the shape to the task (low per-step error → deeper; high → wider), then apply the role the shape demands — a wide scaffold needs a held-out re-eval (#2/#10), a deep one needs checkpoints (#10c). (Low-α 8k-rep sim; the σ-insensitivity corrected a pre-reg.)

## IV. How the researcher (human or loop) should behave
11. **Measure before keep.** Treat every method/finding/self-edit as a hypothesis; validate on held-out (later) data and roll back what doesn't hold. The loop did this twice (cycles 26, 37 overturned over-claimed n=1–2 findings). *Zero rollbacks is a warning sign, not a badge.*
12. **Replicate before believing; survival is necessary but not sufficient.** Borderline winners regress; a finding can survive a fresh seed on the same code yet be an artifact — pair survival with an *independent* re-implementation / perturbation (cycle 31), and prefer a low-α check (the cycle-57 closed form beat the cycle-19 fresh-seed survival).
13. **Don't trust the auto-verdict, or your own self-report.** Read the raw numbers; a script's printed conclusion has been wrong repeatedly (caught ~8 times). Agents (and loops) are overconfident about their own outputs — that's why external verification exists.
14. **Report honestly:** error bars + n + confidence on every claim; distinguish "observed" from "predicted"; report failed pre-regs and negatives faithfully (the discipline's receipts); sims are upper bounds for naive selectors (capable agents partly self-correct, cycle 36) — say so.
15. **Self-improvement is rigor, not rate** (cycle 43, 58-cycle audit): the loop's output was linear (~0.8 findings/cycle, not accelerating); what improved was its self-correction. Describe a self-improving system as "more self-correcting over time," never "faster" — claiming throughput-acceleration would be the exact winner's-curse-on-self it warns against.
16. **Verification is an HONEST READ of the generator's ceiling, NOT a ceiling-raiser** (cycle 118): you can only select a candidate that exists, so the outcome ceiling is set by the GENERATOR (priors/approaches), not the verifier. For OUTCOME the generator has ~10× verification's leverage (a great verifier on a weak generator yields an honest "this is mediocre" — valuable to KNOW, still mediocre). So: invest in the GENERATOR to raise the ceiling; invest in VERIFICATION to know it honestly (remove the winner's-curse delusion, which is large at every generator quality). Verification is about knowing-it's-real, not making-it-real — the generate-side complement to this whole playbook. **And TUNE the generator's VARIANCE (diversity/temperature) to the FLEET SIZE (cycle 119): small fleet → RELIABLE (high-mean) generator; large fleet → WILD (high-variance, "swing for the fences" — best-of-K hits the tail, c57). Counterintuitively a wild generator at scale is DOUBLY good — higher ceiling AND easier to verify (its brilliant outlier stands out → smaller winner's curse, gap∝1/√(σ_gen²+σ_obs²)), while the reliable generator's homogeneous candidates are the hard-to-rank ones needing verification most. So at fleet scale: high-diversity generator + verification, which compound.**
17. **KEEP YOUR OPTIONS OPEN — don't commit irrevocably under noisy judgment** (cycle 122): irrevocable SEQUENTIAL selection (commit now, can't re-call) is dominated by BATCH (generate in parallel, KEEP all, re-eval the finalists) at every noise level — because irrevocability both MISSES the best you've seen (the secretary problem's ~63% miss, even at zero noise) AND forecloses the re-evaluation that fixes the winner's curse (the c7 fix REQUIRES kept candidates). Since research evaluation is always noisy, optionality has large value; the worst architecture forces early irrevocable commitment (cf c102 drift, c117 the cost of having committed).
